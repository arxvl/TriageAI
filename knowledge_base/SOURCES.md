# Provenance notes

Where an entry needs more about its sources than `entries/<slug>.yaml` can hold — a caveat
about what the cited source does and does not support, or a second reference consulted
alongside the one in `source:` — it is recorded here, under the entry's slug.

The entry files themselves carry only the fields the importer declares (see
[`README.md`](README.md)). Anything else in an entry file is rejected by name, so this file
is where the rest of the curator's research lives. Nothing here is imported or stored in
the database; it is read by the team and by whoever verifies an entry before approving it
(see [`APPROVALS.md`](APPROVALS.md)).

All content is **English only** (FR-18).

---

## actions-red

**Source note**

Red denotes immediate priority in Ruys et al. (2012). The immediate
escalation and preparation principles are consistent with Thomovsky and
Ilie's discussion of emergency triage. These are staff workflow actions,
not a substitute for clinical assessment.

**Supporting sources**

- Basic triage in dogs and cats: Part I — Canadian Veterinary Journal —
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10783570/

## actions-orange

**Source note**

Orange denotes very urgent priority in Ruys et al. (2012). Prompt
escalation and preparation are consistent with the emergency-triage
principles discussed by Thomovsky and Ilie. These actions do not specify
a clinical treatment or replace reassessment by veterinary staff.

**Supporting sources**

- Basic triage in dogs and cats: Part I — Canadian Veterinary Journal —
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10783570/

---

## actions-yellow

**Source note**

Yellow denotes urgent priority in the veterinary triage framework. The
listed front-desk actions are procedural workflow suggestions; the cited
sources do not prescribe these exact administrative steps.

---

## actions-green

**Source note**

Green denotes standard priority in Ruys et al. (2012). The specific
registration, waiting, and communication actions above are procedural
suggestions rather than clinical recommendations established by the study.

---

## actions-blue

**Source note**

BLUE is described as non-urgent in the broader five-category framework,
but BLUE was not included in the Ruys et al. (2012) study. The cited
Thomovsky and Ilie Part I article's Figure 1 presents RED, ORANGE, YELLOW,
and GREEN. BLUE actions here are procedural suggestions and are not
validated by those sources.

**Supporting sources**

- Basic triage in dogs and cats: Part I — Canadian Veterinary Journal —
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10783570/
