Case flow (ADR-05, ADR-08, ADR-09, ADR-10)

W-03 Intake→De-identify→Red-flag screen→Extraction (LLM)→Retrieval (pgvector)→Generation (LLM)→Safety validator→W-02 Queue→W-04 Review→Decision

Dashed boxes are the only steps that call an external service, and they receive de-identified text only (ADR-10). The pipeline runs as a background job; the browser polls every 15 s (ADR-08). The safety validator can raise the category but never lower it (ADR-09).

On failure at any stage the case becomes Manual triage required (W-06) and is never lost (NFR-09).

Roles and what they may do (ADR-11, BR-01)

RoleMay do
Intake StaffCreate cases, view the queue and history, see red-flag alerts. Cannot confirm, adjust or finalise a category; W-04 is read-only.
Veterinary ReviewerEverything above, plus confirm, adjust, manually triage, add notes and close cases. The decision always overrides the AI.
+ KB approvalA Veterinary Reviewer flagged can_approve_kb (licensed veterinarian only, BR-04) approves knowledge-base entries and red-flag rules in W-08.
AdministratorUsers (W-09), knowledge-base drafting (W-08), evaluation runs (W-10), exports, read-only audit access. Cannot decide a category.

Urgency categories (IR-03)

Colour is never used alone: every badge carries the category name and its target waiting time.

RED Immediate ORANGE ≤ 15 min YELLOW ≤ 30–60 min GREEN ≤ 120 min BLUE ≤ 240 min MANUAL no AI category

Queue order (FR-29): Red first, then cases with no category (manual or still processing), then Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue; oldest first within a group.

A dashed chip marks AI output that no human has accepted yet. The label “AI Recommendation – requires staff confirmation” stays on screen until a reviewer decides (IR-04).

Decision support only – not a diagnosis. The final category is always decided by veterinary staff (BR-09).

Red-flag signs – notify a Veterinary Reviewer immediately

  • Not breathing or struggling to breathe
  • Collapsed or unresponsive
  • Seizure happening now
  • Bleeding that will not stop
  • Male cat straining with no urine
  • Possible poison or medication swallowed
  • Pale, white, or bluish gums

Final list to be validated by the veterinary reviewer (TBD-4).

Tips for a useful description

Ask when it started, how often it happens, whether it is getting worse, and whether the pet is eating and drinking.
